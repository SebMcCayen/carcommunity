# iOS live-session location quality

Dedicated live-sharing and drive-recording CoreLocation providers filter samples before fan-out. Foreground map/search/crown positioning and Mapbox Navigation use their existing sources. The filter adds no sensor, permission, subscription, backend deployment, or persisted raw GPS data.

Android uses the fused high-accuracy provider (normally 5-second updates) and LivePositionQuality rejects coarse or implausible live-publisher fixes. Its publisher accuracy ceiling is 200 m. iOS previously delivered every CoreLocation sample to publishing and recording; its recorder only rejected segments over 55.6 m/s when calculating distance. Repeated small position errors therefore accumulated into a plausible-looking route while parked.

The dedicated iOS providers now request best-for-navigation accuracy. Samples require known horizontal uncertainty <=50 m, age <=15 seconds (at most 5 seconds clock skew), drawable coordinates and increasing timestamps. Jumps implying >55.6 m/s are refused without poisoning the next good fix. The tighter 50 m limit serves recorded distance, rather than just marker usability. Reduced-accuracy or poor indoor reception may consequently leave live speed unknown and the public marker stale instead of inventing movement; the ordinary foreground map can still locate the user.

Positive speed requires usable CoreLocation speedAccuracy (<=1.5 m/s) and a reading distinguishable from zero. Stationary evidence holds the accepted coordinate. Leaving a parked position requires displacement beyond the 10-50 m uncertainty radius and another corroborating sample over >=2 seconds. A 1 Hz stream retains its first consistent candidate until that window matures, including when the seed has worse accuracy than later fixes. A precise measured zero is stationary evidence; positive speed near zero stays unknown so genuine slow traffic can still be confirmed by displacement. A positive first sample cannot bypass movement confirmation. Sustained driving follows accepted samples directly. GPS recovery after a >15-second gap corroborates a relocated position rather than pinning the old anchor forever. This is a conservative accuracy policy: uncertain slow starts can be delayed or undercounted; a prolonged GPS gap cannot reconstruct the path taken through it.

Held positions keep a fresh timestamp for live-sharing heartbeats, while the recorder ignores identical coordinates. Thus movement accumulated during the uncertainty window is not compressed into the final two seconds and turned into a false top speed. Existing journal restoration remains lossless. Save requests retain the real session end time even when no new route point was accepted while parked.

Apple defines [horizontalAccuracy](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/cllocation/horizontalaccuracy) as a position uncertainty radius and [speedAccuracy](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/cllocation/speedaccuracy) as speed accuracy in m/s. Unknown or invalid accuracy is not a trustworthy zero.

Regression coverage includes two minutes of parked jitter (over 900 m before filtering, zero after), uncertain and misleading positive speed, accurate stationary readings, coarse/stale/invalid fixes, highway motion, start/stop/resume, creeping without speed, 1 Hz delivery, isolated jumps, out-of-order fixes, GPS recovery and native CoreLocation uncertainty mapping. CI must additionally pass the complete existing native suites and real Mapbox SDK build. Physical acceptance in the next TestFlight build must include parking outdoors, a normal drive, a stop, restarting movement and GPS loss/recovery. No real trips or production writes are performed by these tests.
